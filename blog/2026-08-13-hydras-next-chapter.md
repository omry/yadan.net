In 2019, while working at Facebook, I started Hydra to make it easier to build
and configure complex applications. Today, I am happy to share that Hydra has
moved from Meta stewardship to an independent home under the Hydra Ecosystem
GitHub organization.

After I left Facebook at the end of 2021, the team maintaining Hydra gradually
dispersed and development slowed. Adoption continued, however, and people kept
building remarkable things around the project.

<!-- truncate -->

In recent months, I have returned to actively maintaining Hydra and OmegaConf.
The number of open issues and pull requests has fallen substantially,
maintenance is active again, and completing Hydra 1.4 is the next major
priority.

The move gives Hydra an independent home for future decisions about releases,
governance, infrastructure, security, and community processes. OmegaConf will
also move from my GitHub account into the Hydra Ecosystem organization, giving
both projects a shared long-term home.

I am grateful to everyone at Meta who helped make this unusual transfer
possible, and to Hydra's maintainers, contributors, and users for sustaining
the project over the years.
